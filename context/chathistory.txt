Uh, what, how do I up- update the firmware on this cam?

This is almost always because the screen layout in Windows doesn’t match how your monitors are physically placed.

    Right-click the desktop and choose Display settings.
    Click Identify. Numbers appear on each screen, so you can see which is 1 and which is 2.
    In the diagram at the top, drag the screen boxes so they match how the monitors actually sit. If screen 2 is on your right, put its box on the right of box 1, and make the edges touch.
    If the boxes are touching only at a corner, the mouse can’t cross. Slide one box up or down until they share part of an edge.
    Click Apply.
    Scroll down and check that Multiple displays is set to Extend these displays, not Duplicate.

If that doesn’t fix it:

    Press Win + P and pick Extend.
    Make sure the second screen isn’t showing as “Disconnected” or “Not detected”. If it is, click Detect, then check the cable.
    If the mouse crosses but gets stuck at the edge, the two screens may have very different resolutions or scaling. Try setting both to the same scaling, such as 100%.

A “timeout” on refresh just means the router’s web page didn’t get a reply in time. It’s common on cheap routers and doesn’t mean anything is broken.

    Click OK, wait about 10 seconds, and hit Refresh again.
    If it keeps failing, reload the page (F5) or log out and back in, then reopen the client list.
    If it’s still stuck, skip the router and use Fing on your iPad or phone. It scans the network directly and doesn’t depend on the router’s page.
