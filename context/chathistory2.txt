Your output is already useful though. “Reply from 192.168.0.21: Destination host unreachable” is your own PC answering (.21 is karabaja in the DHCP table, so probably your machine). It means Windows asked the network who has .22 and nobody replied. So rtthread is offline right now, not just blocking pings. Either:

    The camera was already powered off when you pinged, which would make it the camera, or
    The lease is stale and rtthread is some other device that’s no longer connected.

To tell which, power the camera on, wait about a minute, and run:

ping 192.168.0.22
arp -a | findstr 192.168.0.22

    If it replies, shut the camera down and ping again. If the replies stop, it’s the camera. The arp line also shows its MAC, which you can match to the router table (00:04:B2:09:85:B6).
    If it never replies even with the camera on, the camera has probably taken a different IP. Refresh the DHCP table and look for a new entry. Also check whether the camera is on 2.4G, since some routers isolate Wi-Fi clients from the wired LAN.

Once it replies, run nmap -p 554,8554,80,8899 192.168.0.22 from your PC. If you don’t have nmap on Windows, Test-NetConnection 192.168.0.22 -Port 554 in PowerShell checks one port at a time.

Two things to keep in mind when reading it:

    A lease isn’t a live connection. A device stays listed until its lease expires, even if it’s powered off, so a camera you’ve shut down will still appear. Pinging is more reliable than this table for telling what’s online.
    Interface isn’t very informative. Ethernet_Port#4 covers the Raspberry Pi, your PC, chuangmi and two iPhones, so that port is probably an access point or switch uplink and doesn’t tell you which device is wired.

To pin down the camera, power it on and wait about a minute. Reload the DHCP page and look for an entry that is new or whose lease timer has just reset to near the maximum. Then run ping on that IP and arp -a and send me the output. Still-unplugged devices will be stuck counting down, while the camera should show a fresh lease.
